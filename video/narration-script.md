# Narration script: How Compostito works

Timed to the captions in `compostito-explainer.mp4` (about 2 min 40 s). Read one line per caption window. A calm pace is about 150 words a minute, and the timings leave room for that. On-screen text that isn't narrated is listed in *italics*.

## Opening  (0:00.0 – 0:05.5)

*How Compostito works · Food waste in. Heat and fertiliser out.*

## 01 · What compost is  (0:05.5 – 0:26.5)

`00:07` Compost is what happens when microbes eat our food and garden waste.

`00:12` Bacteria and fungi break down carbon-rich browns and nitrogen-rich greens, ideally mixed about 25–30 : 1.

`00:19` With oxygen, they turn it into CO₂, water, heat and humus, the dark, stable part of good soil.

## 02 · Aerobic vs anaerobic  (0:26.5 – 0:47.5)

`00:28` Microbes can work with oxygen (aerobic) or without it (anaerobic). The difference matters.

`00:33` Aerobic: fast, hot (55–70 °C) and almost odourless. Carbon leaves as CO₂ and most nitrogen stays in the compost.

`00:40` Anaerobic: slow and cool. A wet, airless pile gives off methane, about 28× as warming as CO₂, and loses nitrogen as ammonia.

## 03 · Meet Compostito  (0:47.5 – 1:10.5)

`00:49` Meet Compostito: a home composter that runs each phase in its own chamber, stacked on stilts.

`00:55` Waste goes in at the top. Mesh walls and two vents let air rise through the pile: cool air in low, warm air out high.

`01:02` Every week or so, material moves down a chamber: A → B → C → D. C and D are half size, because the pile shrinks by 20–50 %.

## 04 · Microbes make heat  (1:10.5 – 1:30.5)

`01:12` As the microbes feed, they multiply, and their metabolism releases heat.

`01:17` Every kilogram of organic matter they break down releases about 18 MJ, roughly 5 kWh.

`01:23` The pile insulates itself, so heat builds up faster than it escapes: from 15 °C to over 60 °C in a few days.

## 05 · The four phases  (1:30.5 – 1:51.5)

`01:32` That rise and fall gives the four phases of composting.

`01:36` Mesophilic (A), then thermophilic (B, 55–70 °C): heat-loving microbes take over, and the heat kills pathogens and weed seeds.

`01:43` Cooling (C) and maturation (D): fungi and slower microbes finish the job. Each turning brings fresh oxygen back in.

## 06 · From heat to the farm  (1:51.5 – 2:16.5)

`01:53` In chamber B, water flows through an aluminium coil and picks up the heat.

`01:58` But compost passes heat on poorly: 99.5 % of the resistance is on the compost side, so a buried pipe gets only about 50 W.

`02:06` Scale fixes it. On a farm, fans push air through the pile, and an air-to-water exchanger sends the heat to dairy hot water, barns and greenhouses.

## 07 · A better fertiliser  (2:16.5 – 2:33.5)

`02:18` After a few weeks of maturation, what comes out of chamber D is dark, crumbly compost.

`02:24` Because it was made with oxygen, it is stable and sanitised, holds its nutrients, and adds organic matter that keeps water and feeds soil life.
