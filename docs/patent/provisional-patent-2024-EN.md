# Energy Composting System with Integrated Boiler for Renewable Heat Generation

*English translation of the French provisional patent application drafted by Maxime Kadri in January 2024 ([original PDF](provisional-patent-2024-FR.pdf)). The provisional application was not extended into a full patent and has lapsed. The author releases the text under CC-BY-4.0, and the design it describes under CERN-OHL-P v2. Headings and structure follow the original. The translator's notes are in italics.*

## 1. Title of the invention

"Energy Composting System with Integrated Boiler for Renewable Heat Generation"

## 2. Introduction

This document concerns a novel composting system combined with a boiler, designed to meet current environmental challenges by bringing together the principles of composting and heat production. The assembly aims to optimise the efficiency of the composting process while harvesting the heat it releases. It helps reduce organic waste and produces a renewable source of energy.

### 2.1 Context and objectives

The invention answers the environmental emergency and the growing need for sustainable solutions with a new approach to organic waste management, and offers an ecological alternative for producing heat. Composting, a biological method of decomposing organic matter, is at the heart of the initiative. It reduces waste naturally and efficiently while producing a valuable organic soil amendment.

### 2.2 Potential advantages

The invention has several potential advantages. First, the optimised composting system meets the essential conditions for producing compost, with particular attention to the ideal carbon/nitrogen ratio of the materials, moisture, oxygen and heat. Second, the boiler design recovers and uses the heat generated during composting, providing a renewable and sustainable energy source.

### 2.3 Specific objectives of the invention

The invention specifically aims to design an integrated composting and boiler system with the following objectives:

- Optimise every phase of composting, from the mesophilic phase to maturation.
- Provide an aerobic, moisture-retaining structure for optimal performance.
- Develop an efficient heat exchanger built into the core of the compost, for maximum heat recovery.

In summary, the invention takes a holistic approach that combines organic waste management with energy production, contributing to the circular economy and to more sustainable practices.

## 3. Principle of the composting process

The system rests on advanced composting principles that optimise the decomposition of organic matter while generating heat for the associated boiler. Each phase of the process is designed around the essential conditions for compost production: nutrient supply, moisture, oxygen and heat.

### 3.1 Nutrient supply (carbon and nitrogen)

The micro-organisms responsible for composting feed on a balanced mix of carbon (C) and nitrogen (N). At the start of the process the C/N ratio must lie between 20:1 and 40:1, with an optimum between 25:1 and 30:1. Example materials and their C/N ratios include fresh green waste and garden residues. The invention proposes an optimal mix of fresh green waste and garden residues to keep the ratio ideal.

### 3.2 Moisture

Moisture content is a critical composting parameter. It must lie between 40% and 65%, with an optimum of 50% to 60%. Water can be added as needed during the active phases to keep the balance right for efficient decomposition.

### 3.3 Oxygen

Compost needs enough oxygen to support microbial activity. A minimum of 5% oxygen is recommended, with an optimum of around 10%. These levels are maintained by the specific design of the chambers and of the aerobic structure.

### 3.4 Heat

When the ideal C/N ratio, moisture and oxygen are maintained, composting goes through distinct thermal phases. The mesophilic and thermophilic phases raise the temperature to specific levels (40-45 °C and 55-70 °C respectively), supporting efficient microbial activity. Cooling and maturation phases follow and complete the composting cycle.

In summary, the composting principle of the invention brings these essential conditions together into an optimal environment for decomposing organic matter, generating heat and producing high-quality compost.

## 4. Boiler layout

The boiler is laid out specifically to optimise the composting process and to exploit the heat it generates efficiently. The layout includes several key elements that maximise use of the heat produced and integrate the boiler with the overall composting process.

### 4.1 Stacked chambers for the composting phases

The boiler consists of a stack of chambers, each holding a different phase of composting. Each chamber is designed for one particular phase, from mesophilic to maturation. This chamber design makes it possible to manage the thermal and environmental conditions each stage needs.

### 4.2 Aerobic structure for optimal performance

The boiler has an aerobic structure that ensures effective ventilation throughout composting. Inner chambers made of plastic mesh allow optimal air circulation and support the microbial activity needed to break down organic matter.

### 4.3 Moisture-retaining structure

The boiler includes a structure that retains the moisture needed for composting. The bottoms of the chambers are made of solid material (plastic) to hold water, which keeps moisture conditions optimal throughout the process.

### 4.4 Heat exchanger for energy recovery

A heat exchanger recovers the heat released by composting, so the generated heat can be used efficiently as a renewable energy source. The exchanger is fully embedded in the core of the compost, maximising the heat-exchange surface.

The layout (stacked chambers, aerobic structure, moisture retention and heat exchanger) is designed to optimise the composting process while capturing the energy produced efficiently.

## 5. Chambers dividing the composting phases

This section concerns the design of the chambers that hold and manage the different phases of composting. Each chamber is arranged to create the environment its stage requires, ensuring efficient decomposition of the organic matter.

### 5.1 Access hatch for nutrients

Each chamber has an access hatch for adding the nutrients the composting process needs. This allows precise control of what the micro-organisms are fed, which supports optimal metabolic activity, particularly during the mesophilic and thermophilic phases.

### 5.2 Composting phases and associated temperatures

The chambers are adapted to the different composting phases, each with its own temperature range:

- **Mesophilic phase (A):** metabolic activity raises the temperature to 40-45 °C.
- **Thermophilic phase (B):** metabolic activity raises the temperature to 55-70 °C.
- **Cooling phase (C):** once the carbon and nitrogen are consumed, the temperature falls back to 40-45 °C.
- **Maturation phase (D):** the temperature returns to ambient.

### 5.3 Volume reduction after degradation

Chambers C and D are half the volume, because the material has shrunk after degradation. This optimises the use of space and improves the overall efficiency of the system.

### 5.4 Chamber materials and structure

The chambers are built as a closed timber structure on stilts. This gives a solid, raised base that avoids ground moisture problems, also helps protect against soil-borne pathogens, and supports proper handling of the organic waste.

In summary, the chambers dividing the composting phases are an essential feature of the invention. They allow precise control of the conditions each stage needs in order to optimise the decomposition of organic matter.

## 6. Aerobic structure and moisture retention

The boiler's structure includes specific elements that promote optimal aeration and adequate moisture retention. Together they create an environment where composting micro-organisms thrive while moisture stays at optimal levels.

### 6.1 Inner chambers of plastic mesh

Inner chambers of plastic mesh reinforce the aerobic structure. They provide optimal air circulation and constant ventilation, so the decomposing organic matter stays properly oxygenated.

### 6.2 Natural ventilation: low vent (VB) and high vent (VH)

The boiler has a natural ventilation system with a low vent (VB, *ventilation basse*) and a high vent (VH, *ventilation haute*). The low vent draws air in from the bottom of the chambers, and the high vent exhausts stale air, constantly renewing the oxygen the microbes need.

### 6.3 Solid (plastic) chamber bottoms for water retention

To retain enough moisture, the chamber bottoms are made of solid material, in this case plastic. This conserves the water composting needs and keeps moisture optimal for the micro-organisms and the decomposition process.

### 6.4 Rainwater recovery feeding chamber B

To complement moisture retention, a rainwater recovery system feeds chamber B specifically. It provides a constant water supply and flexibility in maintaining moisture throughout every phase.

In short, the aerobic structure and moisture retention built into the boiler are fundamental to the invention, allowing precise control of the conditions needed for successful composting.

## 7. Thermal output of the chambers

A crucial feature of the invention is the chambers' ability to generate optimal thermal output throughout composting. The chambers create a controlled microclimate that maintains suitable temperatures, especially in winter, so decomposition continues.

### 7.1 A microclimate matched to the degradation process

The chambers are designed to create a microclimate that keeps internal temperatures matched to the needs of degrading organic matter. Microbial activity stays constant even in unfavourable weather.

### 7.2 Heat exchanger to recover the released heat

A heat exchanger integrated into the chambers efficiently recovers the heat released by composting. The recovered heat is channelled into the overall gas heating system, providing a renewable energy source.

### 7.3 Temperature management in winter

To keep temperatures adequate in winter, the chambers are sized to create a favourable microclimate with optimal thermal output. Microorganism activity stays sufficient even when ambient temperatures are lower.

### 7.4 Volume reduction for effective thermal management

The chambers' volume halves after the material degrades. This allows more effective management of thermal output and optimal use of the available space.

In summary, the thermal output of the chambers is a fundamental aspect of the invention. It allows effective use of the heat generated during composting and creates a controlled microclimate for optimal thermal performance.

## 8. Heat exchanger

The central element of the invention is a heat exchanger designed to recover the heat released by composting as fully as possible. The exchanger is an essential component of the boiler: it contributes substantially to producing renewable energy and ensures the generated heat is used efficiently.

### 8.1 Maximising compost core temperature

The exchanger's main objective is to exploit the temperature of the compost core, where microbial activity is most intense. To do this, it is fully embedded in the core of the compost, giving direct and efficient heat transfer.

### 8.2 Increasing the heat-exchange surface

The exchanger increases the heat-exchange surface between itself and the compost core by reducing the diameter of the pipe network while multiplying the number of networks. This optimises recovery of the heat composting generates.

### 8.3 Avoiding stagnation inside the exchanger

To avoid stagnation inside the exchanger, the exchanger is immersed in a volume of high thermal inertia. This volume stores heat, holds it, and releases it diffusely, so the generated heat can be used continuously and efficiently.

### 8.4 External protection by a concrete casing

A concrete casing protects the exchanger from outside. Concrete was chosen for its low cost, heat resistance and high thermal inertia. It acts as a physical barrier that protects the exchanger during transport and shields it reliably from the elements.

### 8.5 Versatile aluminium exchanger

The exchanger itself is made of aluminium, a light and highly conductive material. Its exact shape will be determined from experiments that establish the optimal dimensions of the compost core. This design allows the exchanger to be used in a range of conditions.

In conclusion, the heat exchanger is a key innovation of the invention. It contributes substantially to recovering and using the heat generated during composting.

## 9. Control probes

An essential component of the invention is a set of control probes that monitor and regulate conditions inside the boiler. They enable optimal control of composting, consistent performance, and an ideal environment for decomposition.

### 9.1 Dual temperature and humidity probe

Each chamber, and chamber B in particular, has a dual temperature and humidity probe. The probes give real-time monitoring of internal conditions and a precise view of how temperature and humidity evolve during composting.

### 9.2 Digital display on the front of the unit

The probe data is shown on a digital display on the front of the equipment, so users can easily monitor key parameters and manage composting proactively.

### 9.3 Strategic role in chamber B

The control probe in chamber B plays a strategic role by closely monitoring this crucial phase. It helps keep conditions ideal for the transition from the thermophilic phase to cooling, ensuring optimal decomposition.

### 9.4 Adaptable to different composting configurations

The probes adapt to different composting configurations. Whether the feedstock is fresh green waste, garden residues or other organic matter, the probes allow parameters to be adjusted to the material being processed.

### 9.5 Maintaining the heat supply

When the feedstock is renewed, the thermal inertia of the material surrounding the exchanger lets the control probe keep the heat supply going through the transition. Composting stays stable even while the feedstock changes.

In summary, the control probes are crucial to the success of the invention. They allow close monitoring and precise regulation of conditions and contribute to consistent composting performance.

## 10. Implementation of the boiler

This section covers the practical side of the boiler: installation, operation, and integration into existing systems. It shows the invention's practical applicability.

### 10.1 Supplement to an existing installation

The boiler is designed as a supplement to an existing installation, so it integrates with existing heating systems and extends the invention's range of application. It can be used modularly, alongside other heat sources, or as a standalone unit.

### 10.2 Compatibility with common temperature regimes

Most heating installations run on specific temperature regimes, such as 60/40 °C and 70/50 °C. The boiler is designed for these common regimes, so it is compatible with conventional heating systems on the market.

### 10.3 Pre-heating the heating return water

Positioned as a by-pass on the network, the boiler pre-heats the heating return water. It raises the water temperature before the water reaches the existing boiler room, improving the overall efficiency of the heating system.

### 10.4 Ease of installation and maintenance

The boiler is designed to be easy to install and maintain. Mounting it as a by-pass makes integration into existing systems easy, and access to essential components is optimised for quick, efficient maintenance.

### 10.5 Contribution to equipment management

The boiler is designed to contribute to the overall management of the equipment. Its by-pass position allows composting to be managed without disturbing existing systems, giving operational flexibility and longevity.

In conclusion, implementation is a key step in the practicality and efficiency of the invention. Its well-considered integration and operation make it an adaptable solution that can be adopted in a variety of settings.

## 11. Conclusion

The invention in this provisional application is a significant advance in composting and renewable energy production. It combines new composting principles with a clever boiler design to offer a complete, efficient solution for managing organic waste while generating sustainable energy.

The key to the innovation is the synergy between an advanced composting process and smart recovery of the released heat. By following composting principles, in particular maintaining optimal nutrients, moisture, oxygen and heat, the system creates an environment that favours microbial activity and ensures fast, efficient decomposition of organic matter.

The strategic layout of the boiler makes the invention a holistic and sustainable solution: separate chambers for each composting phase, an aerobic structure for optimal ventilation, and an exchanger that recovers the released heat.

The practical implementation shows the boiler adapts to a variety of existing configurations, making it a versatile addition to existing heating systems. It is easy to integrate and effective as a supplement, which positions it as a leading technology in energy composting.

In summary, the invention offers a new answer to today's challenges of organic waste management and renewable energy production. The combined benefits of sustainable waste management and energy production make the system a significant contribution to the transition toward more environmentally friendly practices. *(The original's closing sentence about protecting the invention through the provisional application is omitted, since the application has lapsed and the design is now open.)*
